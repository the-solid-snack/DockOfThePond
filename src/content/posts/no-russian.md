Twenty klicks of forced march through the wilderness with rifle and full kit makes for a long day. The obvious risks barring the use of anything resembling a trodden path do nothing to help. Nor the fact that there isn't a single square inch of this goddamn country that isn't at a 45-degree angle to the horizon, every bit of floor more keen than the last to pulverise your ankle. But Gribbs refusing to shut up since dawn about his fucking Korean dramas, to the point where I'm wondering where he gets the ability to face the terrain and go on with his litany at the same time, that truly is the jewel in the toe ring on the foot that has been kicking me in the balls all day. Nothing, apart from the fact he would be the only one to carry my carcass should things come to that, holds my tongue from straight out ordering him to shut his trap. That and perhaps the fact the wee bastard probably wouldn't anyway.

Keeping all senses alert – barring auditory channels for obvious reason, banking on the odds of a gang of PKM-wielding goats jumping us here being rather low – I try to focus on the landscape to nurse my dwindling patience. I've said it before: the high ridges, barren valleys giving way to large pockets of jungle-lush green…

Politics aside, this place would make a decent holiday resort.

Movement, corner of my eye. A tree branch waving against the wind, emerging from behind a massive boulder about ten metres left of the line. My left fist goes up instinctively at the same time as the right tightens around my L119A2. The scrote shuts up immediately and for a second I curse myself for not thinking of that as a valid – if temporary – way to nail his trap shut hours ago.

Judging from how quick he drops, he's seen it too. I signal I'm going left and instruct him to the right. He gestures back – God, I hate when the wee cunt argues – suggesting he follows me instead. I confirm my initial order via a universally understandable hand gesture familiar to motorists and football fans alike, and start to progress.

Could be a man waiting to ambush us as much as a shepherd picking up a fruit from a low branch. Either would mean having to take someone down – quietly. We're still a few klicks away from the target location, but not far enough that I'd risk gunshots alerting the anthill to the incoming boot.

I lower my weapon and draw the blade from my vest, advancing silently. Feeling my accelerated heartbeat, I notice even the birds are suddenly quiet, like they can feel the sudden rise in air pressure.

Visual check: the kid'll be in place in a second. I'm about to break cover and acquire the target when the crack of a dry stick – virtually deafening considering the ambient silence – alerts the whole bloody neighbourhood.

I launch myself around the boulder, blade raised ready to strike anything close. Meanwhile the kid goes around the other way, muzzle up in the unlikely event of multiple targets being there waiting for us.

The next moment I'm on my arse, left cheek bruising against the dry stones, my chest feeling like it's been stamped flat by a bloke wearing size 15s. Instinctively the blade in my hand shoots forward and bounces off something hard and coarse, the bearer of which bellows in my ears, voice full of a mix of sheer terror and some desperate anger.

Adrenaline is flooding my system as I'm trying to make sense of whatever the fuck it is my eyeballs report. After a second or two, I realise that the overall shape doesn't match what you'd expect of a military-age male. Too small. Too grey. Too… wait. Furry?

Fucking assault goats, man.

The little sack of shit rammed me head first and owes its life to sheer luck that my knife skidded on a horn. Dazed, it bolts down the track as I get up on my feet. I feel the kid's grin burning a hole in my back. Fair enough, from where he's standing the whole scene was probably close to Benny Hill material. To my credit, CQB training didn't cover encounters with charging farm animals.

Recovering my senses, I sharpen back up. Thankfully the vest dampened the impact of an animal launched at ballistic speed. It's a long shot, but the presence of that daft beast hints at a herder somewhere who might have heard us and, assuming, would be heading straight to the source of the racket. "We're moving outta here," I signal.
