Nobody in France believes me when I describe this. I have tried at three separate
dinner parties and watched the same expression cross the same faces, somewhere
between pity and concern, as if I had confessed to putting ice in wine.

The dish, if we are being generous with the word, is a bread roll, butter, and a
packet of crisps. That is all of it. There is no sauce, no garnish, and no
technique to speak of.

## Why it works

The argument I keep failing to make in French is about contrast. Every part of it
is doing a job that the other parts cannot do.

- The bread is soft and slightly sweet, and it does nothing except get out of the way.
- The butter is there for salt and fat, and it must be cold enough to sit in ridges.
- The crisps supply the only interesting texture in the entire construction.

Crush the packet slightly before you open it. Not to dust — you want shards. The
whole point is the noise.

> A sandwich should surprise your teeth at least once.
>
> <cite>My own defence, offered at a dinner party, to silence</cite>

<p>Cheese and onion. This is not a matter of taste. Salt and vinegar turns the butter strange, and anything smoky argues with the bread.</p>

## What my grandmother would say

She would say that bread is a vehicle for things that took effort, and that a
potato already fried by a factory is not effort. She would be right about the
effort and wrong about the outcome, which is the most French possible way to lose
an argument.

I have made her ratatouille perhaps forty times. It takes two hours and it is
better than this in every measurable way. I still eat more crisp sandwiches.
